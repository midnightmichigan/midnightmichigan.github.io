The problem this solves

Most small sellers track land contract payments in a notebook, a shoebox of money order receipts, or their head. That works right up until the buyer disputes a balance, a payoff is requested for a refinance, or tax time asks how much of last year's money was interest. Then the notebook becomes the most expensive paper in the house.

This kit replaces it with one folder per property: a ledger where every payment is split into interest and principal the day it arrives, receipts for the buyer, an escrow tracker for taxes and insurance, the annual statement your buyer expects in January, and the notice letters already written for the day a payment runs late.
